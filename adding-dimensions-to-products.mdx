Product dimensions drive volume-based packing and any condition using **Product Length**, **Width**, **Height**, or **Volume**. Shopify has no native dimension field, so ShipMagic stores them for you.

<Note>
  All dimensions are in **millimetres**.
</Note>

## Where to enter them

Open the **Products** page in the app. Every product is listed with editable **Length**, **Breadth**, and **Height** fields. Enter values and save.

For a handful of products this is the fastest route. For a full catalogue, use the CSV flow below.

## Bulk upload by CSV

<Steps>
  <Step title="Download the template">
    On the Products page, choose **Bulk upload dimensions**, then **Download CSV format**. This gives you a file with your products already listed.

    <Note>
      The export is emailed to you rather than downloaded directly. It goes to the **Contact Email** set on the app's Settings page — change it there if the mail should go elsewhere.
    </Note>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the dimensions">
    Add length, breadth, and height in millimetres for each row. Leave rows blank if you don't have data for them yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload the file">
    Back on the Products page, open **Bulk upload dimensions** and drop the completed `.csv` into the upload area.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Export first, fill in the file you get back, and upload that. Building a CSV from scratch means matching product identifiers by hand, which is where mistakes creep in.
</Tip>

## What dimensions are used for

**Volume-based packing.** The **By items volume** distribution in the [packing algorithm](/shipping-rates/types-of-rates/carrier-rates/3-d-boxing-algorithm) cannot work without them.

**Carrier rate accuracy.** Carriers price on dimensional weight as well as actual weight. Wrong dimensions mean quotes that don't match your invoice.

**Rate conditions.** [Product Length / Width / Height / Volume](/cart) conditions let you route oversized items to a freight rate.

## Measure the shipped size

Enter the dimensions of the product **as it ships**, including its own packaging — not the bare item.

<Warning>
  Dimensional weight is where carriers make their margin. A product measured without its retail box can be significantly under-quoted, and the difference lands on your invoice rather than the customer's.
</Warning>

## Products with no dimensions

A product with no dimensions contributes nothing to a volume calculation. On a **By items volume** rate this quietly understates parcel size, producing a rate that's too low rather than an error you'd notice.

<Tip>
  If your catalogue is only partly measured, use **By items count** or **By weight** packing until the data is complete. They degrade more gracefully than volume packing does.
</Tip>

New products start with no dimensions, so make measuring part of adding a product.
