title: "Exclusive Rules"
description: "Stop lower-priority rules from being evaluated once a rule matches"

By default, every rule whose conditions match contributes its rates. **Exclusivity** changes that: an exclusive rule that matches stops evaluation there.

## Inclusive vs. Exclusive

Every rule has an **Exclusivity** setting with two choices:

| Setting | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| **Inclusive** (default) | Lower-priority rules are still evaluated and their rates added. |
| **Exclusive** | If this rule matches, all lower-priority rules are skipped. |

The app states it directly on the rule form: _"If an 'Exclusive' rule is satisfied, all lower-priority rules are skipped from evaluation."_

## Priority decides what "lower" means

Exclusivity is meaningless without [priority](/shipping-rates/basic-shipping-rate). Rules are evaluated in **ascending** order — 1 before 2 before 10 — and an exclusive match stops everything after it.

Rules with a **higher** priority number are the ones skipped. Anything with a lower number has already run.

<Warning>
  An exclusive rule at priority 1 that matches broadly will suppress your entire rule set. Give exclusive rules narrow conditions, or place them after the rules that must always run.
</Warning>

## When to use it

**Overrides.** A rule for a special case that should replace normal pricing rather than add to it — hazardous goods that must ship freight, at a freight price, regardless of what else is in the cart.

**Customer tiers.** A wholesale rule that should give its own rates without the retail rules also contributing.

**Free shipping promotions.** A promotional free-shipping rule that must not have a surcharge rule adding to it afterwards.

```text
Rule 1 — Free shipping promo (Priority 10, Exclusive)
Condition 1: Cart Subtotal <> Greater Than or Equal <> 150
Rate: Standard Shipping, Fixed rate, 0

Rule 2 — Standard pricing (Priority 20, Inclusive)
Condition 1: Cart Subtotal <> Less Than <> 150
Rate: Standard Shipping, Fixed rate, 6.95
```

## Exclusivity vs. blending

These solve overlapping problems from different angles, and it's worth being clear which you want.

- [**Blending**](/rate-blending) lets every matching rule contribute, then decides how to combine the results — add them, or take the highest or lowest.
- **Exclusivity** stops the later rules producing anything at all.

<Tip>
  If the answer is "the other rules shouldn't even run", use exclusivity. If it's "they should run, but I only want the cheapest", use lowest-rate blending. Exclusivity is the blunter and more predictable of the two.
</Tip>

## A common mistake

Marking several rules exclusive rarely does what people expect. Only the **first** exclusive rule to match has any effect — everything after it is skipped, exclusive or not.

<Note>
  If two exclusive rules both seem to apply, the one with the lower priority number wins and the other never runs. Check your priority ordering before concluding a rule is broken.
</Note>

## Testing

Exclusivity is invisible until rules overlap, so test the overlap deliberately.

1. Build a cart matching **only** the exclusive rule — confirm its rates appear
2. Build a cart matching the exclusive rule **and** a lower-priority rule — confirm only the exclusive rule's rates appear
3. Build a cart matching only the lower-priority rule — confirm it still works
