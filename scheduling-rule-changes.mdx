title: "Scheduling Rule Changes"
description: "Activate or deactivate a rule automatically at a future date and time"
icon: "calendar-days"
---

Rather than remembering to switch a rule on at midnight, you can schedule it. Both **shipping rules** and **shipping customizations** support scheduled activation and deactivation.

This is how you run holiday pricing, a promotional free-shipping window, or a temporary surcharge without being at your desk when it starts and ends.

## Adding a schedule

<Steps>
  <Step title="Save the rule first">
    Schedules attach to a saved rule. On a brand-new rule you'll see _"Save this rule first to add schedules"_ until you do.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the Schedule card">
    It sits in the sidebar of the rule or customization editor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a schedule">
    Choose an **Action** — **Activate** or **Deactivate** — then set the **Date** and **Time** (hour, minute, AM/PM).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    The schedule appears in the list with a badge showing whether it will activate or deactivate.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Timezone

Schedules run in your **store's timezone**, which is displayed at the bottom of the schedule dialog.

<Warning>
  Check the timezone shown before setting a time, particularly if you operate in a different one from your store's configured timezone. A sale starting at "midnight" means midnight where the store thinks it is.
</Warning>

## Pairing activation and deactivation

A time-boxed change needs two schedules on the same rule — one to switch it on and one to switch it off.

```text
Rule: Black Friday free shipping
Schedule 1: Activate   — 28 November, 12:00 AM
Schedule 2: Deactivate — 02 December, 11:30 PM
```

<Tip>
  Create both at the same time. A promotion that starts automatically and then needs a manual switch-off is a promotion that runs three days too long.
</Tip>

Minutes are available in half-hour steps, so times land on the hour or half hour.

## Limits

<Note>
  Each rule or customization can hold up to **3 pending schedules**. If you reach the limit, cancel an existing schedule before adding another.
</Note>

The limit counts _pending_ schedules, so ones that have already run free up space.

## Planning a seasonal calendar

With three pending schedules per rule, one rule can cover a start, an end, and one more transition. For a longer calendar, use several rules — one per period — each with its own activate and deactivate pair.

<Tip>
  Build the seasonal rule ahead of time and leave it **Inactive**, with a scheduled activation. It's tested, dormant, and switches itself on when the period starts.
</Tip>

## Verifying a schedule

A schedule only proves itself when it fires, which is usually the worst moment to discover a problem.

1. Test the rule now, by activating it manually and checking rates at checkout
2. Deactivate it again
3. Add the schedule
4. Confirm the pending schedule and its badge look right

<Warning>
  Scheduling changes whether the rule is **active** — it does not change any of the rule's other settings. If you need different prices for a period, that's a separate rule with its own schedule, not an edit to the existing one.
</Warning>
